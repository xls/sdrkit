# M17

M17 amateur digital voice: callsigns, text, positions, SMS packets; Codec 2 speech through a plug-in. Transmits voice from the microphone (Codec 2 3200) and SMS or data packets.

Stack id: `m17`. Group: Digital voice.

Signal types: M17.

## Transmit

The stack sends M17 by the public M17 specification: a voice stream with Codec 2 at 3200 bit/s from the microphone, and
packets of SMS text or data. The engine setting `transmit_allowed` is the only switch; the stack adds no other gate.

Commands:

- `ptt` with `on` true starts a voice stream from the setting `callsign` to the setting `destination`; `ptt` with `on`
  false ends it. The stream also ends with `halt` and at the time-out (`tx_timeout`).
- `send` sends a text as an SMS packet (protocol byte 5, the text, a zero byte, the CRC), up to 821 bytes.
- `send_data` sends bytes as a packet with the protocol byte given (0 to 255), up to 822 bytes.
- `halt` ends any transmission after the frame on air.

Settings: `callsign` (own call sign, the source; empty by default), `destination` (a call sign, or `@ALL` for broadcast),
`can` (channel access number, 0 to 15), `voice_mode` (Codec 2 3200), `meta_text` (up to 52 characters in the META
field of a voice stream), `tx_split` (transmit offset) and `tx_timeout`. A setting change applies to the next
transmission. While `callsign` is empty, every transmit command is refused with an error that names the setting. A call
sign outside the M17 alphabet is refused when it is set.

The signal:

- 4FSK at 4800 symbols/s, root raised cosine frequency pulse with roll-off 0.5, deviation 2.4 kHz for the outer symbols
  and 0.8 kHz for the inner symbols. The 99 % power bandwidth is within 9 kHz.
- A transmission is the preamble (192 symbols of +3 and -3), the link setup frame, then the stream or packet frames, then
  the end of transmission (the word `555D` over 192 symbols). Each frame is 40 ms.
- A stream frame carries a LICH chunk (5 bytes of the link setup frame and its counter in four Golay words), the frame
  number from 0, and two Codec 2 frames. Push-to-talk off, `halt` and the time-out end the stream after the frame on air:
  the next frame carries the end-of-stream bit, then the end of transmission follows.
- A packet frame carries 25 bytes and 6 bits: the end flag and the bytes in use in the last frame, else the frame counter.
  A packet has at most 33 frames.
- The TYPE field of the link setup frame has the layout that the receiver of the stack decodes: bit 0 stream, bits 1 and
  2 the data type (2 voice 3200, 1 data for a packet), bits 3 and 4 the encryption type (0), bits 5 and 6 the META kind
  (0 text), bits 7 to 10 the channel access number, bit 11 signed (0). The receiver of the stack names version 2.0 of the
  specification. That version 2.0 keeps this layout is not confirmed: this is an open point.
- The carrier rises and falls with a 5 ms raised cosine. The magnitude is 0.9 of full scale, constant over the burst.
- The output has the rate of the session; its samples carry the signal at 0 Hz, and its centre is the transmit frequency
  (the listening frequency plus `tx_split`). Every transmission ends with an end mark. On a live radio whose host times
  bursts, the first sample states its time on air.

Speech comes in at the microphone input of the component `m17.transmit` (`mic`, mono audio of any rate, converted to
8 kHz). The encoder needs the eleven Codec 2 tables of the 3200 bit/s rate: `lsp_cbd_0.f32` to `lsp_cbd_9.f32` and
`nlp_fir_0.f32`. The stack reads them from the folder `codec2` of the host's storage and checks each file by its SHA-256;
a host can also hand them over with `M17Transmit::set_codebooks`.
Without them `ptt` is refused with the name of the missing table; packets need no tables.

Results: `transmitting`, `tx_freq_hz` (while transmitting), `tx_kind` (voice or packet) and `tx_frames` (the stream or
packet frames sent in the transmission on air, or in the last one).

Not sent: the 1600 bit/s form with data (the library has no Codec 2 1600 encoder), a GNSS position in the META field,
pre-coded speech frames from the host, encryption and signed streams. The receive audio is muted while the stack sends.
