# QO-100

QO-100 through a Ku LNB: beacon lock and LNB correction, the transponders' signals, conversations and DATV carriers. Sends SSB voice on the narrowband uplink.

Stack id: `qo100`. Group: Satellite.

## Settings: notes

Advanced settings. The owner works without any of them set.

## Transmit

The stack sends SSB voice (push-to-talk) on the uplink of the narrowband transponder, in upper sideband, from the audio of
the host. The engine setting `transmit_allowed` is the only switch: with it off, `ptt` is refused and a transmission on air
ends at once. The stack does not send the callsign: say it.

You need an amateur licence to transmit. The band plan of the transponder (AMSAT-DL) applies: send only in the segment for
SSB, with a bandwidth of at most 2.7 kHz, listen first on the downlink, and keep your signal on the downlink weaker than the PSK beacon at
10489.750 MHz. Without a licence, use the sender only into a dummy load, on a cable or in a shielded enclosure.

### Commands and settings

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| `ptt` | With `on` true, sends the speech of the microphone input until `ptt` with `on` false or the time-out. With `on` false, ends the transmission with a 5 ms fall and the end mark. |

The settings, in the section Transmit: `tx_level` (the peak magnitude, 0.05 to 0.9 of full scale, default 0.4) and
`tx_timeout` (push-to-talk ends after this time on air, 1 to 3600 s, default 180). A change of these applies to the next
transmission. A change of `dial_hz` moves the uplink at once and ends a transmission on air.

### The uplink frequency

The transponder moves the uplink to the downlink by a fixed translation of 8089.5 MHz (the value is not confirmed by a
public document of the operator). The stack sends at the setting `dial_hz` less the translation: 2400.150 MHz for the dial
10489.650 MHz. The sent signal is at the same place on the downlink as the USB signal the dial listens to. The sender
follows the setting `dial_hz`, not a conversation that auto-listen follows. The radio, or an up-converter after it, must
transmit at 2.4 GHz; the result field `tx_freq_hz` holds the uplink frequency at all times.

### Results

`transmitting`, `tx_freq_hz`, and during a transmission `tx_time_s` (the time on air), `tx_mic_dbfs` (the peak of the
microphone audio), `tx_peak_db` (the peak of the modulation against the peak level), `tx_level_db` (the gain of the level
control) and `tx_limit_db` (the most gain reduction of the limiter).

The beacon rule: `own_to_beacon_db` is the level of the strongest signal in the 2.7 kHz above the dial less the level of
the PSK beacon, both as the peak of a bin of the narrowband spectrum. While you send, that signal is your own downlink:
lower `tx_level` or the power of the radio until the value is below 0 dB. With nothing on the dial the value is the
level of the noise peaks there. The field has no value while the beacon is not found. The stack only measures: it does not lower the level and does not end a transmission.
The receive side keeps running during a transmission, so the radio must receive and send at the same time.

### On air

The sender writes into the transmit sink as many samples in each pass as the pass brings in, at the session rate. On a live
radio whose host gives a transmit lead, the first sample states its time on air. The last sample carries the end mark. The
transmission rises and falls with a 5 ms raised cosine.

### The signal

The speech processor is the one of the general stack: a 250 Hz high-pass, the audio low-pass at 2700 Hz, a slow level
control, and look-ahead limiters that keep the peak at or below `tx_level`. A complex band-pass filter passes the upper sideband,
300 to 2700 Hz above the suppressed carrier. Measured on the sender at 2 Msps: with a 1 kHz tone, the lower sideband is
94 dB and the carrier more than 100 dB below the upper sideband; with test audio of equal tones from 100 Hz to 5 kHz, the
occupied bandwidth (99 % of the power) is 2594 Hz, from 244 to 2838 Hz above the uplink dial.
