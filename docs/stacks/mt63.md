## Settings: notes

Advanced settings. The owner works without any of them set.

## Transmit

The stack sends typed text at the three widths with both interleaves. The engine setting `transmit_allowed` is the only switch:
with it off, `send` and `tune` are refused and a burst on air ends at once.

### Commands and settings

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| `send` | Checks the whole text and queues it. A burst starts when none is on air; text sent during a burst joins it. At most 600 characters wait. |
| `stop` | Drops the text waiting and ends the signal with a 10 ms fade and the end mark. |
| `tune` | A steady carrier at the audio centre for `tx_tune_limit` seconds (1 to 60, default 10). |

A signal that does not fit 200 to 3800 Hz above the dial at the settings chosen refuses `send` and `tune`.

The sender shares `bandwidth` and `interleave` with the receiver, so both sides use one value; `auto` sends 1000 Hz and the
short interleave. Its own settings are `tx_low_hz` (the lowest carrier, 200 to 3000 Hz, default 500 Hz), `tx_level` (the peak,
0.05 to 0.9 of full scale, default 0.5) and `tx_tune_limit` (default 10 s). The tune carrier is at the centre of the band,
`tx_low_hz` + width / 2. The signal is on the upper sideband.

Characters: bytes 0 to 255 as typed, each sent as the byte AND 127. A character above 255 refuses the whole text, and the
refusal names it.

### Results and events

`tx_sending` (a burst or the tune carrier is on air), `tx_sent` (characters of the burst coded so far) and `tx_waiting`. The
event `hfkbd.tx` says when text started a burst, was queued, finished (with the reason: stopped, the transmitter is off, the
format changed) or was refused (with the reason).

### On air

The sender writes into the transmit sink as many samples in each pass as the pass brings in, at any session rate from 12 kHz
up. On a live radio whose host gives a transmit lead, the first sample states its time on air: the time of the pass that writes
it plus the lead and two passes. The last sample carries the end mark. A burst is framed by 0.15 s of silence before and 0.1 s
after, with a 10 ms raised-cosine fade at each end of the signal. 64 carriers add up: the peak is `tx_level` (a look-ahead limiter of 2 ms holds every sample at it or
under), the mean power about 10 dB under it (10.5 dB measured). The transmitter chain must be linear.

### The signal

- 64 carriers from `tx_low_hz` up, width / 64 apart; each starts at phase 0 at the first sample of the burst and runs on.
  One character per symbol (T = 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 s for 500, 1000 and 2000 Hz).
- Carrier i sends bit i of the 64-chip Walsh word of the character entered d(i) symbols before (d from the steps of the short or
  the long interleave, the largest 31 and 63). A bit 1 keeps the carrier's sign, a bit 0 reverses it. Before the first
  character the carriers send random chips from a fixed seed.
- Each symbol is shaped by the stack's own pulse over 2.56 symbols, so three symbols overlap.
- A burst: NUL for max(5.5 s, L + 16 symbols), the text, L + 12 NUL and the end of the last pulse (L = 32 or 64).

### Checks

- The signs of NUL (every carrier reverses at every symbol), the Walsh word of "C", the delays of carriers 0 to 15 of both
  interleaves, and the preamble and tail of a burst.
- Every width and interleave: the stack's own receiver reads the text exactly without noise, with nothing before it, and whole
  at 30 dB in 2.5 kHz. Over the seeds 1 to 10: MT63-1000 short at 4 dB 10 of 10, long at 2 dB 9 of 10; MT63-500 and MT63-2000
  at 8 dB 10 of 10 with both interleaves.
- A run of `@` (no sign changes) shows the 64 carrier lines within 0.5 Hz of their places.
- The 99 % power bandwidth: 493.7, 988.8 and 1977.5 Hz; the opposite sideband more than 72 dB down.
- Also covered: the frame of a burst, `stop`, `tune`, the gate and the refused character.

### Settled and open points

- The lowest carrier is at 500 Hz at every width by default, as the public description gives (500 to 1000, 1500 or 2500 Hz).
- Before the first character the carriers send random chips (the behaviour the specification states) and not the chips of NUL
  (its proposal): with NUL every carrier reverses in step, the preamble has a peak 21 dB over its mean power, and the receiver
  lost the first characters of a burst at 1000 and 2000 Hz.
- No public document gives the Walsh row order, the interleave steps, the pulse or the start phases; those follow the
  specification. The pulse is the stack's own; no public document gives the pulse of the mode, so the spectrum of other
  stations may differ in detail.
- `stop` ends the signal at once with the 10 ms fade, as in every keyboard sender, not at the end of a symbol.
